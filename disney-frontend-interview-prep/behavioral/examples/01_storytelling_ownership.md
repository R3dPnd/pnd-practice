# STAR template: storytelling / ownership of your journey

Example prompt shape: "walk me through a project you owned end to end" / "tell me about
a time you drove something to completion without being asked" / "what's a decision you
made that you'd stand behind today."

Fill this in with a real story — don't fabricate one. Good candidates: a feature you
scoped and shipped with minimal direction, a piece of tech debt you decided (on your
own initiative) was worth paying down, a tool or process you built because nobody asked
you to but it was clearly needed.

- **Situation**: _(1–2 sentences — what was the project/problem, and why did it land on
  you specifically?)_
- **Task**: _(what were you personally responsible for — be specific about scope, not
  "the team's goal.")_
- **Action**: _(the decisions YOU made and why — Disney's own framing wants "the
  creativity you brought," so don't undersell a clever or unconventional call you made.)_
- **Result**: _(the outcome, ideally with a number or a concrete artifact — a metric
  moved, a system shipped, a process adopted by others.)_
- **Learning**: _(the concrete change: e.g. "I now write a one-page scope doc before
  starting anything ambiguous, because that project taught me how much time gets lost
  to re-litigating scope mid-build.")_

## Common failure mode to avoid

Don't describe a project where you were just executing someone else's fully-specified
plan — that's not an ownership story, no matter how well you executed it. The
interviewer is listening for evidence that YOU made judgment calls under uncertainty,
not that you're a reliable executor of other people's decisions.
