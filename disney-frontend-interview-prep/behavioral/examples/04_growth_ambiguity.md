# STAR template: growth / handling ambiguity or setback

Example prompt shape: "tell me about a time you failed" / "tell me about a production
incident you were involved in" / "tell me about a time you had to solve a problem with
very little direction."

Fill this in with a real story — don't fabricate one. A reported real Disney technical-
round question was specifically **"tell me about a production incident/regret,"** so
have a genuine incident ready, not a hypothetical or a heavily-sanitized non-failure.
Other good candidates: a project where the requirements were genuinely unclear and you
had to define scope yourself, a technical bet that didn't pay off.

- **Situation**: _(1–2 sentences — what broke, or what was ambiguous, and what was the
  stakes/impact?)_
- **Task**: _(what were you responsible for in the moment — diagnosing it, fixing it,
  communicating it, deciding how to proceed?)_
- **Action**: _(what you actually did — be honest about the mistake if it's a failure
  story; a story with no real mistake in it isn't believable as a failure story.)_
- **Result**: _(how it was resolved, including the actual cost — downtime, a missed
  deadline, a redo — don't sand off the rough edges.)_
- **Learning**: _(the concrete change: e.g. "I now add a health-check alert before
  shipping anything that touches the publish pipeline, because that incident happened
  because we had no alerting on that path at all.")_

## Common failure mode to avoid

Don't pick a "failure" that's secretly a humblebrag (e.g., "I worked too hard and
burned out my team with my high standards"). Interviewers have heard that one; it reads
as evasive rather than reflective. Pick something where you actually made a call that,
in hindsight, was wrong, and say so plainly.
