# STAR template: ownership of a domain area

Example prompt shape: "tell me about a system or area you owned" / "what's something you
were the go-to person for" / "walk me through a piece of the architecture you're
responsible for end to end."

JD language this maps to: *"an understanding of the importance of project ownership"*
and *"take ownership of one or more of the team's domain areas."* This is subtly
different from a generic "project you led" story — they want evidence of **sustained**
ownership of a bounded piece of a larger system: you're the person who gets pinged when
that area breaks, who makes the roadmap calls for it, who knows its edge cases better
than anyone else on the team.

- **Situation**: _(what was the system/service/area, how big was the team around it, and
  how did it become "yours" specifically — inherited, volunteered, only one who
  understood it?)_
- **Task**: _(what were you responsible for — uptime? feature roadmap? being the
  technical point of contact for other teams integrating with it?)_
- **Action**: _(a decision you made *because* you owned it, that someone without that
  ownership context wouldn't have made — e.g., you pushed back on a feature request
  because you knew it would violate an invariant the rest of the team didn't know about;
  you proactively paid down debt nobody assigned you.)_
- **Result**: _(a concrete outcome — reliability improved, other teams could self-serve
  against your API without pinging you, a metric moved.)_
- **Learning**: _(what ownership taught you that execution-only work doesn't — e.g., "I
  started writing a one-page decision log for this service so ownership context survives
  even if I'm out for a week.")_

## Common failure mode to avoid

Don't describe a one-time project with a start and end date as if it were ownership —
ownership stories are about the *ongoing* relationship with a system (weeks/months/
longer), including the boring parts: on-call, incremental hardening, saying no to scope
creep that would hurt the system's health. If your best story is a single sprint, pick a
different one or reframe around what happened *after* launch.
